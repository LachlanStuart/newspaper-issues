Drug Discovery, Molecular Design & Simulation

EasyNano optimizes epitope-targeted nanobody CDRs through differentiable ESMFold2 distograms

EasyNano is a rapid nanobody CDR-redesign pipeline that optimizes CDR residue logits through an ESMFold2-Fast distogram proxy. It uses an explicit CDR-to-epitope proximity loss and a structure-prior mask intended to prevent framework pose drift, with reported ipTM gains for weak binders such as Ty1/RBD and KN035/PD-L1 while preserving constraints such as disulfides.

E-CloudBind uses electron-density point clouds for robust protein–ligand affinity prediction

E-CloudBind reframes affinity prediction around electron-density point clouds and density-overlap interaction graphs rather than atomic-distance cutoffs. Reported results emphasize robustness to crystallographic-resolution changes and AlphaFold2 structure substitution, with screening examples for PBP1A, SARS-CoV-2 Mpro and BCL-2.

MDForge designs molecular-dynamics pipelines with LLM agents and sparse simulator feedback

MDForge is an LLM-agent system for synthesizing complete molecular-dynamics workflows and improving them from sparse terminal simulator rewards. Its PRISM mechanism decomposes failures into structured diagnostics and typed critiques from force-field, sampling and analysis specialists, with benchmarks including SAMPL host–guest free-energy tasks and a prospective CB[7] binding case.

APCyc designs cyclic peptides with automated, pocket-conditioned cyclization topology

APCyc is a target-aware latent diffusion model for cyclic peptide design that treats cyclization chemistry and linkage sites as design variables. It predicts residue-level cyclization topology, injects linkage probabilities into an SE(3)-equivariant denoiser, and supports property-guided generation for affinity, permeability, protease resistance, solubility and immunogenicity.

TheraPep-AI classifies therapeutic peptides with augmented negative sampling

TheraPep-AI is a sequence-only multi-label classifier for 48 therapeutic peptide functions. The key idea is a hard synthetic-decoy ladder for negative sampling, aimed at lowering false positives; the report cites a 2.1% false-positive rate on peptide-like synthetic negatives while preserving useful multi-label performance and sparse-filter interpretability.

Post-hoc uncertainty estimator filters molecular diffusion model samples

This work estimates uncertainty for pretrained 3D molecular diffusion models by perturbing denoiser parameters with a Laplace-style approximation and aggregating variance in noise predictions across denoising trajectories. On QM9, the score correlates with stability and validity better than diffusion NLL and can help test-time filtering, though the reported benefit does not transfer to GEOM-Drugs.

MolGram augments molecular language models with local n-gram memory

MolGram adds conditional local n-gram memory to SMILES Transformers while retaining character tokenization. Using hash-based n-gram retrieval, contextual gating and local convolution, it reportedly improves molecule generation, forward reaction prediction and retrosynthesis across GPT and T5-style backbones.

Protein Models, Enzymes & Biosecurity

Sparse autoencoder features from ESMC predict enzyme function with interpretable concepts

The paper uses sparse autoencoder features from ESMC-6B as interpretable protein signatures for EC-number prediction. Simple probes over SAE activations reportedly approach or exceed BLASTp on microbial SwissProt enzyme benchmarks, retain coverage for no-hit cases, and surface millions-scale dark enzyme-like candidates from ESM Atlas representatives.

Viral proteins reveal a dominant nativeness axis in protein language model embeddings

A study of protein language model embeddings finds a strong principal-component “nativeness” axis aligned with reconstruction perplexity, placing viral proteins between cellular proteins and random or shuffled controls. The pattern appears across ESM, ProGen2 and EvoDiff families, while viral-specific information remains linearly accessible for detection and biosecurity-style evaluation.

DeepRHP learns latent design spaces for random heteropolymers as protein mimics

DeepRHP is a semi-supervised hybrid VAE for random heteropolymer sequence ensembles, coupling sequence reconstruction with reconstruction of local hydrophilic–lipophilic balance features. It maps synthetic polymer libraries and reduced protein sequences into a shared latent space to suggest compositions resembling target membrane proteins such as Aquaporin Z.

RNA, Transcriptomics & Virtual Cells

m6A-FORM is an RNA m6A foundation model for peak-aware site calling and regulator binding

m6A-FORM is a transformer pretrained on millions of MeRIP-seq peak-derived RNA windows and fine-tuned for NER-style within-peak m6A site labeling. The model is also reused for m6A regulator binding and decay-outcome prediction, with attribution analyses connecting sites to RBP context and tissue-conserved methylation patterns.

TifBERT is a self-supervised foundation model for bulk RNA-seq representations

TifBERT converts unordered expression profiles into sample-specific gene sequences using TF-IDF ordering, then pretrains with masked gene modeling over whole-transcriptome sliding windows. The method targets normalization-robust bulk RNA-seq representation learning without predefined landmark genes, external gene embeddings or projection-based compression.

OCOO-T is a minimalist virtual-cell model for transcriptional perturbation-response prediction. It uses a plain Transformer stack directly on continuous gene-expression profiles and formulates perturbation response as continuous-time denoising conditioned on perturbation and cellular covariates.

Single-Cell, Multiomics & Regulation

ANCHOR infers allelic and isoform structure from single-cell long-read RNA-seq

ANCHOR is a haplotype-aware single-cell long-read RNA-seq method that discovers expressed heterozygous variants de novo, assigns molecules to parental haplotypes with a pair HMM, and aggregates UMI-level evidence with a beta-binomial model that can abstain under ambiguity. Isoforms are defined from observed splice structures rather than only reference transcript assignment.

TMO learns cell-state-dependent regulatory lags from single-cell multiomic data

Temporal Multi-Omics, or TMO, is a deep learning framework for signed, cell-state-conditional regulatory lags using asymmetric cross-modal attention. It projects RNA and ATAC into latent components, tokenizes each cell, and biases attention with a data-driven lag prior from sliding-window cross-correlation.
