LLM wrapper reportedly lifts robot-policy success without fine-tuning

An LLM-driven agent wrapped around the π0.5 robotics policy handles planning, execution checks, and recovery from failures. The researchers report that this architecture raised real-robot success from 16.7% to 97.3% without additional policy fine-tuning.

Coding-agent token overhead traced to accumulated context

Sebastian Raschka reports that Claude Code consumed roughly two to three times as many tokens as several other agent harnesses at comparable success rates. His log analysis attributes most of the gap to messages, tool records, command output, and file contents repeatedly supplied as input—not to longer answers. One 25-turn run used about 578,000 input tokens versus 4,500 output tokens.

Activation steering reduces harmful driving judgments

Goodfire says it traced an LLM’s drunk-driving judgments to a small group of impairment-related activation features. Amplifying five features fourfold reduced harmful endorsements on held-out prompts from 30 of 48 to 20 of 48, with no reported increase in refusals for sober-user scenarios.

Structural-database bias may alter protein-model generations

Evidence highlighted by Diego del Alamo suggests that thermostable archaeal proteins are overrepresented in structural databases because they are easier to characterize experimentally. Models trained on those databases may consequently generate sequences with measurably shifted properties.

seqproc offers a declarative language for sequencing reads

seqproc interprets and transforms sequencing reads with the Extended Fragment Geometry Description Language. Its ANTISEQUENCE engine composes matching and transformation primitives as directed acyclic graphs, aiming to make complex processing specifications concise and reusable.

map3C unifies single-cell Hi-C processing and SV annotation

map3C converts locally aligned paired-end reads into chromatin contacts using Pairtools algorithms. Its workflow also includes multimapping trimming, quality-control reports, artifact filtering, and annotation of contacts potentially induced by structural variants, including likely breakpoints.

Explore the project post

SynFlow makes synteny and structural variation interactive

SynFlow is a browser-based environment for computation and visualization across whole-genome assemblies. It turns dense static synteny plots into shareable views that can be filtered and reordered to inspect features such as translocations.
