Front Page Picks

Bio / Omics

SPARE proposes auditable recovery of single-cell RNA-seq zeros

SPARE, short for Selective Partition-Aware Recovery of Expression, is a single-cell RNA-seq zero-recovery method with an unusually explicit provenance story. It tracks whether matrix entries were measured non-zeros, selected observed zeros, untouched zeros, or marker-vetoed zeros, then keeps that intervention map through recovery and audit steps.

The interesting part is not just “better imputation”; it is leakage control and accountability in downstream analyses, which matters a lot if recovered expression values are going to feed models or biological claims.

https://x.com/razoralign/status/2063413932384293090

AI Interpretability

Can activation verbalizers surface an internal chain of thought?

A LessWrong post evaluates whether activation verbalizers can expose a model’s single-forward-pass reasoning. The early read: they may be useful, but are not yet reliable enough to treat as transparent access to internal chain-of-thought.

This is worth tracking because it sits right at the uncomfortable boundary between interpretability, elicitation, and “are we just making another model tell us plausible stories?”

https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QQQAcKuWK6k98FivY/can-activation-verbalizers-surface-an-internal-chain-of-1

Spatial Biology

SciCore-Omics: tri-modal foundation model for spatial biology

SciCore-Omics is described as a tri-modal foundation model joining histology, spatial transcriptomics, and language. The key question is whether it can actually reason over spatial context, rather than merely aligning separate image, omics, and text embeddings.

If the spatial reasoning is real, this is directly in the “multimodal biological foundation model” lane.

ADAPERT, “Learning Adaptive Perturbation-Conditioned Contexts for Robust Transcriptional Response Prediction,” is a perturbation-response modeling item aimed at predicting transcriptional effects under interventions.
