AI & computing

EmbeddingGemma 2: batching pays differently across modalities

MLX-VLM measurements on an M5 Max report roughly 800 texts per second at batch 32, about 85 audio clips per second peaking around batch 16, 23 images per second with little batching benefit, and nine five-frame video clips per second. A memory follow-up puts text and audio below approximately 3.5 GB at batch 32, versus 14 GB for images and 33 GB for video.

The practical recommendation is to keep image and video batches small. These are reported hardware-specific measurements, not a general performance guarantee.

Genomics, interpretation & evaluation

Mendel learns variation across donors to prioritize regulatory variants

Mendel focuses DNA-model training on variable alleles, comparing the same genomic loci across GTEx donors. The supplied summary reports that residual genotype-prediction difficulty distinguishes fine-mapped regulatory variants from matched controls with AUROC 0.731, and improves elastic-net expression prediction when used as a variant-weighting prior.

The evaluation holds out individuals, not genomic loci. Its genotype accuracy should not be treated as a general variant-effect benchmark.

Mendel research summary

VANDAM adds DNA biophysical priors to foundation-model training

VANDAM combines regional prediction of sequence-derived molecular properties with local DNA-shape features. Across four model families and nine functional-genomics tasks, reported point estimates improve in 29 of 36 pretraining comparisons and 33 of 36 multitask comparisons.

Random and shuffled controls support a contribution from the biological priors rather than extra capacity alone. Those priors are derived from sequence, however, not direct measurements of DNA structure.

VANDAM research summary

AuditPPI separates prediction accuracy from binding mechanisms

AuditPPI turns sparse features from protein language models into interpretable pair fingerprints. Reported AUROC drops from 0.973 on a conventional split to 0.746 under topology-controlled human evaluation and 0.720 on a leakage-controlled benchmark.

Audits find substantial signals from interaction participation and cellular context; apparent interface enrichment often disappears after controlling for solvent exposure. Even protein-disjoint evaluation, the analysis cautions, need not demonstrate partner-specific binding mechanisms.

AuditPPI research summary

SoftGene brings protein-sequence information to gene-set annotation

SoftGene hierarchically pools protein isoforms, genes and gene sets, then feeds learned soft prompts to a frozen local LLM. The summary reports gains on GO and MSigDB annotation benchmarks, including approximately gene-disjoint evaluation.

Ablations suggest sequence embeddings help cellular-component annotations most, with little benefit for some biological-process labels. Attention weights provide candidate explanations, not proof of causal importance.

SoftGene research summary

AmyloCore-ML tests amyloid-core prediction with leakage-aware splits

On a curated set of 69 proteins, AmyloCore-ML distinguishes residues incorporated into experimentally resolved fibril cores from other residues in the same proteins. Protein-grouped cross-validation prevents overlapping-window leakage; a locked ESM-2/ExtraTrees scanner reportedly reaches residue AUROC 0.833 and mean peak-localization error of 12.4 residues.

Classification accuracy does not guarantee precise localization, and unresolved regions are not necessarily incapable of forming cores.

AmyloCore-ML research summary

RipplePLM writes mutation-effect descriptions from sequence changes

RipplePLM combines wild-type–mutant ESM-2 representation differences, contact-guided local and distal attention, and property pseudo-labels to condition descriptions of mutation effects. It reports improved ROUGE-L on temporal and structural-novelty splits of MutaDescribe.

Better text overlap does not establish the factual accuracy of biological explanations or reliability for clinical variant interpretation.

RipplePLM research summary

PIE commentary asks what “unseen perturbation” really means

A methodological comment notes that PIE uses DepMap dependency profiles, themselves experimental perturbation readouts. An “unseen perturbation” can therefore mean an unmeasured transcriptional response to an intervention already characterized in another modality. This qualifies the generalization claim without, on its own, establishing data leakage.

Molecular design & engineering

Proton-PottsMPNN yields experimentally screened pH-responsive binders

Proton-PottsMPNN explicitly represents protonation states during sequence design, optimizing the surrounding environment for a preferred state. A yeast-display screen of 8,407 designs recovered hundreds of pH-dependent PD-L1 binders, with reported transitions around pH 4.0–5.8. Learned selectivity scores separated recovered from unrecovered designs better than the cited PROPKA3 estimates.

The screen demonstrates pH-responsive binding—not therapeutic efficacy or suitability for physiological tumor conditions.

Proton-PottsMPNN research summary

pCoMole edits sequences under multiple objectives and constraints

pCoMole guides variable-length biomolecular sequence editing while applying feasibility constraints to final candidates rather than every intermediate step. Applications include GFP shortening, Cas9 compression and peptidomimetic optimization.

Two shortened GFP variants retained fluorescence in cells. The reported Cas9 PAM-preservation and therapeutic-property results are computational, not demonstrations of editing activity or pharmacology.

pCoMole research summary

IDiom steers disordered regions through sparse-autoencoder features

Trained on 54 million predicted intrinsically disordered regions, IDiom uses an RL-SAE method to reward selected sparse-autoencoder features. The aim is to combine compartment-associated sequence patterns with transcriptional activation or repression features.

The supplied account reports stronger feature activation and predicted localization than steering or supervised fine-tuning. The functional and localization results remain computational predictions, not demonstrated cellular activity.

IDiom research summary

LSCO searches for better synonymous codons without changing the protein

Latent Space Codon Optimization searches a pretrained mRNA model’s latent space using an ensemble expression predictor alongside RNA-stability and naturalness constraints. These constraints seek to reduce off-distribution optimization.

Reported gains on 54 antibodies persist across multiple predictive evaluators. No wet-lab expression validation is described, so higher predicted yield remains a hypothesis.

LSCO research summary

AbRefine combines antibody-framework context with structural inverse folding

AbRefine learns when to combine frozen structural and framework-conditioned antibody models. Matched-versus-shuffled framework controls support a genuine context contribution, with the largest recovery gains where structural information is uncertain and framework information is useful.

Evaluation covers 534 antibodies across three cohorts. Native-sequence recovery and fold-back checks do not demonstrate improved antigen affinity.

AbRefine research summary

CODesign jointly generates binder sequences and all-atom structures

CODesign combines consistency-distilled complex data with inference-time resampling of incompatible residues. The work introduces Codimer, approximately 104,528 distilled dimers, and reports improved computational binder success across protein and small-molecule targets.

Success is defined by structure-predictor confidence and self-consistency thresholds. No experimental binding validation is described.

CODesign research summary

Higher-order molecular grammars expand ring-topology benchmarks

Higher-order Grammar Representation encodes molecular motifs and ring systems as reversible production-rule sequences. The work introduces RingDiv, containing 1.18 million molecules, plus a smaller benchmark to broaden ring-topology coverage.

Reported results include valid generation under RDKit checks and improved molecular-property classification. Computational validity and distributional similarity do not establish synthesizability or drug efficacy.

Deutsch — Post-Quanten-Krypto: BSI besorgt über McEliece

heise online · 06.10.2026 · Artikel lesen

Das Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik rät davon ab, Classic McEliece in neuen Projekten zur quantensicheren Verschlüsselung einzusetzen. Das traditionsreiche Verfahren soll den Schlüsselaustausch auch gegen künftige Quantencomputer absichern. Neue Forschungsarbeiten stellen jedoch seine Widerstandsfähigkeit gegen Angriffe mit gewöhnlichen Computern infrage: Der theoretisch erforderliche Rechenaufwand fällt erheblich geringer aus als bislang angenommen. Praktisch gebrochen ist das Verfahren damit laut Bericht noch nicht.

Für neue Projekte empfiehlt das BSI stattdessen FrodoKEM, ML-KEM oder – nach Veröffentlichung des entsprechenden Standards – HQC. Bestehende Anwendungen müssten nicht zwingend sofort umgestellt werden, insbesondere wenn McEliece mit einem klassischen Verfahren kombiniert wird. Bei dieser hybriden Nutzung bleibt zumindest dessen Schutz erhalten. Die bisherige Empfehlung für McEliece soll in der nächsten Ausgabe der technischen Richtlinie Anfang 2027 geändert werden.

Der Beitrag zieht daraus eine grundsätzliche Lehre für die Umstellung auf Post-Quanten-Kryptografie: Auch standardisierte Verfahren können durch neue Erkenntnisse an Vertrauen verlieren. Systeme sollten deshalb so aufgebaut sein, dass sich einzelne kryptografische Bausteine ohne großen Aufwand austauschen lassen. Diese sogenannte Krypto-Agilität ist nicht bloß eine technische Zusatzoption, sondern eine Absicherung gegen veränderte Sicherheitsannahmen.

Warum lesen: Der Artikel zeigt an einer konkreten Entscheidung des BSI, warum selbst zukunftssichere Verschlüsselung laufend überprüft werden muss und was das für die Planung digitaler Infrastruktur bedeutet.

Français — « Plaider en faveur d’une homologation rapide » : l’Allemagne est prête à accélérer pour autoriser le Tesla FSD en Europe

Numerama · 6 octobre 2026 · Lire l’article

L’Allemagne souhaite accélérer l’autorisation du système d’aide à la conduite FSD de Tesla dans l’ensemble de l’Union européenne. Selon Numerama, le ministre allemand des Transports, Steffen Bilger, a annoncé son soutien à une homologation rapide, alors que le déploiement progresse jusqu’ici pays par pays. Berlin suit le dossier avec Tesla et les autorités compétentes allemandes et néerlandaises : l’enjeu est de passer d’autorisations nationales à un cadre permettant une utilisation plus large dans l’UE.

Un point particulièrement sensible concerne la vitesse. L’article rapporte que le ministère allemand a accepté le principe d’un dépassement pouvant atteindre 10 % de la limitation par le système, au nom d’une circulation plus fluide. Berlin entend défendre cette marge dans la procédure européenne ; il ne s’agit donc pas d’une règle déjà généralisée à toute l’Union. Cette position pose une question concrète : jusqu’où adapter les règles de circulation au fonctionnement des aides à la conduite ?

Le ministère rappelle toutefois que le conducteur conserve entièrement la responsabilité de la conduite. Malgré son nom, le FSD reste présenté comme une assistance qui exige une vigilance humaine, et non comme un dispositif dispensant de surveiller la route. L’intérêt de cette lecture tient à cette articulation entre promesses techniques, décisions réglementaires et responsabilité en cas d’incident.

Pourquoi lire : Un article court pour comprendre comment l’Allemagne pourrait peser sur le déploiement européen des aides à la conduite et sur leurs limites juridiques.

Vocabulaire utile

  • homologation — validation officielle attestant qu’un équipement respecte les exigences nécessaires à son utilisation.
  • système d’aide à la conduite — ensemble de fonctions qui assistent le conducteur sans nécessairement le remplacer ni supprimer sa responsabilité.
  • autorité de régulation — organisme public chargé d’encadrer un secteur et de veiller au respect des règles applicables.

日本語 — 日本の中国進出企業：ピーク時から3割減、26年は1万118社に―帝国データ調べ

nippon.com · 2026-10-07 · 記事を読む

帝国データバンクの調査によると、中国に進出している日本企業は2026年に1万118社となり、ピーク時から約3割減少した。香港とマカオを除いた企業活動を集計したもので、2010年の調査開始以来、最も少ない。記事は日中関係の悪化を背景に、中国ビジネスの縮小が進んでいると伝えている。

前回調査からの2年間で1221社が新規参入した一方、「撤退・所在不明」に分類された企業は4137社に上った。進出企業の構成も変わり、中小企業の割合が下がる一方、中堅・大企業の比率が上昇している。地域別では上海市が依然として最大の集積地だが、企業数は前回より21.5％減少。業種別では製造業が最も多く、卸売業、サービス業が続く。
