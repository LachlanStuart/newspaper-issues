DeepLoop scales the depth of looped Transformers

DeepLoop proposes techniques for keeping weight-shared, looped Transformers stable as their effective depth increases. A follow-up reports that the resulting model surpassed much larger systems on ARC-AGI-1, suggesting that repeated computation may offer another route to capable models beyond simply adding parameters.

“Diffusing Blame” trains dual-stream networks without backpropagation

The method broadcasts task-dependent error signals directly to hidden layers, enabling networks with dedicated excitatory and inhibitory neurons to follow Dale’s principle without conventional backpropagation. Evaluations cover image recognition and backpropagation-free reinforcement learning, including locomotion and Craftax.

Drug Discovery & Molecular Engineering

PeptiVerse unifies affinity and developability prediction for therapeutic peptides

PeptiVerse offers a standardized workflow for predicting hemolysis, permeability, solubility, toxicity, half-life, non-fouling behavior, and protein-binding affinity from amino-acid sequences or SMILES. It combines frozen ESM-2, PeptideCLM, and ChemBERTa embeddings with lightweight property-specific models, supports chemically modified peptides, and supplies uncertainty estimates and standardized datasets.

The evaluation uses similarity-aware splits to reduce leakage. One notable result is that OpenFold3 ipTM reportedly has almost no association with experimentally measured peptide–protein affinity.

Machine learning engineers cross-reactive anti–PD-L1 antibodies with minimal mutations

Researchers paired yeast-display deep mutational scanning with a ConvNeXt-style sequence classifier to make a human anti–PD-L1 antibody bind mouse PD-L1 while preserving human binding. Their search produced variants with only four or five mutations, compared with 13–15 substitutions in conventional DMS-derived leads.

Epitope mapping, structural modeling, cell binding, blockade assays, and a CT26 mouse tumor study provide experimental support for the designs.

Molecular-dynamics datasets support toxicity and binding prediction

The DEMR framework converts molecular-dynamics trajectories into sequences of compact atom-pair contact tensors and processes them with a Transformer. Its accompanying TOX21-MD and DUD-E-MD datasets contain standardized 10-nanosecond trajectories for toxicity endpoints and three binder-identification tasks.

Reported results indicate that dynamics are especially useful for imbalanced toxicity prediction, with roughly ten sampled frames often enough for competitive performance.

Genomics, Immunology & Bioinformatics

AlphaGEM reconstructs genome-scale metabolism with structural alignment and protein language models

AlphaGEM automates metabolic-model construction for non-model organisms through structure-first orthology mapping, PLMSearch, and an ensemble of enzyme-annotation systems. Structural filters reject misleading sequence homologs, while the ensemble looks for divergent proteins that may account for “dark metabolism.”

Benchmarks span fungi, bacteria, mammals, and 332 yeast species, with reported improvements in phenotype prediction and gene–reaction coverage, alongside less reliance on gap filling.

Immune-repertoire generators often trade generalization for memorization

A benchmark of generative models for T-cell and B-cell receptor repertoires examines clone-frequency structure, sequence statistics, connectivity, memorization, innovation, and preservation of subject or phenotype signals.

Many models reproduce clonal expansion by copying dominant training clones. Coverage of unseen TCRs remains low and is largely limited to sequences only one or two mutations away from training data, underscoring the need for standardized AIRR-specific evaluation.

BatchSVG flags spatially variable genes driven by technical bias

BatchSVG compares per-gene binomial-deviance rankings with and without a covariate representing a known technical bias. Genes whose rankings shift substantially are marked as batch-associated and can be removed before downstream spatial analysis, helping prevent artifacts from being mistaken for biological regulation.
