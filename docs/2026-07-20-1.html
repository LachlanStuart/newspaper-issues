Molecular Design

DrugRPG brings chemical and physical constraints into ligand generation

DrugRPG is a pocket-conditioned 3D diffusion framework intended to stop generated ligands from exploiting docking models while violating basic chemistry or steric constraints. It aligns its representation with a large 2D chemical encoder, explicitly models bond types, and applies differentiable clash guidance during sampling.

Across 10,000 generated molecules, the reported results include fewer severe clashes, better redocking consistency, and stronger multi-objective developability than seven comparison methods.

Perturbed Flow Matching aims for faster pocket-conditioned generation

Perturbed Flow Matching modifies flow-matching trajectories using protein-pocket information and coupled atom-type and coordinate signals while preserving endpoint distributions. Its equivariant network models ligand, protein, and cross-molecular interactions, supplemented by bond-length and protein-clash losses.

On CrossDocked2020, the authors report improved docking and geometry metrics as well as a 21.3× valid-molecule generation speedup over the diffusion baseline used in their timing comparison.

Proteins & Enzymes

EnzGFM specializes language modeling for enzyme research

EnzGFM combines Mamba and Transformer components, with pretraining first on UniRef50 and then on curated enzyme sequences. The paper reports gains in kinetic-property prediction, enzyme–reaction retrieval, EC classification, and mutation-effect prediction, including evaluations with low-sequence-identity splits.

An associated agent also prioritized SEAP variants for a CHO-cell experiment. Every candidate unique to its top set reportedly improved activity, with the best reaching 7.13× wild type.

N-terminal model predicts stability differences between protein isoforms

A model trained on roughly 48,000 experimentally measured 23-residue N-termini predicts protein-stability effects beyond the first two residues emphasized by canonical N-end-rule models. It identifies an internal-arginine stabilization pattern and validates graded effects through reporter assays.

Applied to alternative translation starts, the model predicts—and experiments confirm—that short extensions or truncations can substantially change isoform stability by introducing or masking hydrophobic segments.

Biomedical Discovery & Gene Regulation

SemVac uses literature-aware language models to rank vaccine antigens

SemVac scores proteins as protective-antigen candidates using literature-derived functional descriptions rather than sequence or structure alone. The study compares 14 language models on bacterial and cross-kingdom antigen datasets, tests them after removing papers with explicit vaccine keywords, and applies the approach to the Mpox proteome.

One notable result is a practical warning: explicit reasoning increased recall but reduced precision, suggesting that over-reasoning may be costly when each proposed target requires experimental follow-up.

DB-IRES predicts internal ribosome entry sites from RNA sequences

DB-IRES combines densely connected one-dimensional convolutions, a bidirectional GRU, attention, and five-fold soft-voting ensembles to classify internal ribosome entry sites from 174-nucleotide sequences. It was trained on 18,527 sequences from a high-throughput bicistronic assay.

The model reports an AUPR of 0.504 on the imbalanced test set, while motif analysis recovers purine-rich patterns. Fixed-length inputs and uncertain cross-species generalization remain important limitations.
